## Cooperativism vs capitalism

:::compare
### Cooperative costs = Coordination
- Collective bargaining
- Meeting overhead
- Referendum expenses
- Preference alignment
- Conflict resolution
- Capital acquisition
### Capitalist costs = Extraction
- Management surveillance
- Compliance and tracking systems
- Lack of employee motivation due to limited ownership
- Investor relations overhead
- Misalignment between short and long term gain
- DIVIDEND PAYMENTS TO SHAREHOLDERS
:::

## The case

Capitalism has outlived its usefulness. Hyper concentrated wealth in the hands of a tiny minority is an active detriment to the well-being of the many. Fortunately, cooperative business structures can eliminate the excessive wealth inequality (and the imbalance of power) that capitalism created. By fairly distributing ownership amongst those who **produce** value (rather than amongst those who simply supply the upfront capital), civilization can finally solve not just wealth inequality, but arguably every human-made problem on the planet.

Critically, the economic efficiency of cooperative ownership is accelerating, and this acceleration is being powered by the explosive advancement of digital technology over the last two decades.

## Blockchain

Blockchain technology is by and large an untapped resource when it comes to
solving coordination problems. Over the last 10 years or so, it has developed a
stigma that prevents many from looking more deeply at the core technology. Far
beyond simple financial scams, blockchain presents an opportunity for large
swathes of people to achieve consensus more cheaply than ever in history.

The technology's capacity to trustlessly provide voting, governance, data
storage, digital provenance, escrow, payment facilitation, and many other
things **WILL** revolutionize almost every industry as people begin to realize
the applications outside of (degenerate) finance. For marketplace cooperatives
especially, blockchain represents the opportunity to build something that can
economically outcompete an otherwise-equivalent capitalist business.

By dropping coordination costs while shareholder demands for dividends remain
the same, cooperative business structures will edge their way into the market
and slowly take over more and more industries.
