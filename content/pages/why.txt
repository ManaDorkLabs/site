## Cooperativism vs capitalism

- Collective bargaining
- Meeting overhead
- Referendum expenses
- Preference alignment
- Conflict resolution
- Capital access
### Capitalist costs = Extraction
- Management surveillance

## The case

Capitalism has outlived its usefulness. Hyper concentrated wealth in the hands of a tiny minority is an active detriment to the well-being of the many. Fortunately, cooperative business structures can eliminate the excessive wealth inequality (and the imbalance of power) that capitalism created. By fairly distributing ownership amongst those who **produce** value (rather than amongst those who simply supply the upfront capital), civilization can finally solve not just wealth inequality, but arguably every human-made problem on the planet.

Critically, the economic efficiency of cooperative ownership is accelerating, and this acceleration is being powered by the explosive advancement of digital technology over the last two decades.

## Blockchain

Blockchain technology (or distributed ledger technology, DLT) is by and large an untapped resource for solving coordination problems. Blockchain, in its initial incarnation as "decentralized finance", developed a (well deserved) stigma that still prevents many from evaluating the core technology more closely. Far beyond simple financial scams, blockchain presents an opportunity for large swathes of people to **achieve consensus more cheaply than ever in human history**.

Blockchain's capacity to trustlessly faciliate voting, governance, data storage, digital provenance, escrow, payments, and countless other digital activities *will* revolutionize every digital industry as people begin to recognize the applications outside of "crypto". For marketplace cooperatives especially, these capabilities grant the power to build an economic contender to the entrenched pro-capitalist order.

Shareholder demand for dividends will always remain the same, since the demand for profit is driven by greed and thus will always be maximized. Cooperative coordination costs, however, can only ever go down, and as we slash those costs, cooperative business structures will edge their way into the market and eventually take over more and more industries.

As they say: first slowly, then... all at once.
