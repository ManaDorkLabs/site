- Management surveillance
- Compliance and tracking systems

## The Thesis

Capitalism has outlived its usefulness. Despite driving innovation for the last two hundred years, it has caused wealth to become hyper concentrated in the hands of a tiny minority, who are using that wealth to further increase their own power, at the detriment of everyone else. Fortunately, cooperative business structures can eliminate the excessive wealth inequality (and therefore the imbalance of power) that capitalism has generated. By fairly distributing ownership amongst those who **produce** value (rather than amongst those who simply supply the upfront capital), civilization can finally solve not just wealth inequality, but arguably every human-made problem on the planet.

Because a standard corporation is *legally required* to maximize returns for its shareholders, the capitalist demand for extraction is not reducible; if the corporation *can* extract more value, it is *legally obligated* to do so. The often missed extrapolation here is that this legally-mandated shareholder profit comes at the expense of both the employees who work for the business and the customers who purchase from the business. **Shareholder profits represent a Dead Weight Loss tax between producers and consumers.**

Cooperatives, on the other hand, are not built with the same incentives; the owners are not shareholders, but members of the cooperative: sellers, buyers, users, patrons, owners. The cooperative does not exist as a profit maximizing entity in and of itself, but rather as a **cost minimization entity for all of the members who utilize it.**

The key comparison for evaluating which business structure is more efficient then becomes this:
> are the coordination costs of running a cooperative less than the extractive costs of running a capitalist corporation?

Historically, the answer to this has, unfortunately, been **no**. Crucially, though, the economic efficiency of the cooperative structure can be improved, and this improvement is enabled by the explosive advancement of digital technology over the last two decades.

## The Asymmetric Cost Reducer

Blockchain technology (or distributed ledger technology, DLT) is an untapped resource for solving coordination problems. DLT, in its initial incarnation as "decentralized finance", developed a (well deserved) stigma that unfortunately still prevents many from evaluating its use cases more rationally. Far beyond mere financial scams, DLT presents the opportunity for large swathes of people to **achieve consensus more cheaply than ever before in human history**.

DLT's capacity to trustlessly facilitate voting, governance, data storage, digital provenance, escrow, payments, and countless other online activities will inevitably revolutionize every digital industry. For marketplace cooperatives especially, DLT grants the capabilities to build an economic contender worthy of rivalling the entrenched pro-capitalist order.

**As the saying goes: first slowly, then... all at once.**
