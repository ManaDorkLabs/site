The Background

Why cooperatives

Every firm pays to coordinate itself. Cooperatives and capitalist firms just pay in different currencies — and only one of those bills is getting cheaper.

Cooperativism vs capitalism

The honest comparison is shareholder dividends weighed against cooperative coordination costs. Both columns are real. Neither structure is free.

Cooperative costs

  • Collective bargaining
  • Meeting overhead
  • Referendum expenses
  • Preference alignment
  • Conflict resolution
  • Capital acquisition

Capitalist costs

  • Management surveillance
  • Compliance and tracking systems
  • Lack of employee ownership limits motivation
  • Investor relations overhead
  • Misalignment between short term and long term gain
  • Dividend payments

The case

Capitalism may have been the dominant market force for the last 200 years, but today it has outlived its usefulness. Hyper concentrated wealth in the hands of a tiny few is an active detriment to the well-being of the many. We believe that cooperatives are the solution to the excessive wealth inequality — and the imbalance of power — that exists today. If we can figure out how to fairly distribute ownership to those who produce the value, rather than that ownership remaining amongst those who simply supplied the upfront capital, then we can begin to start truly solving not only wealth inequality, but in turn almost every human-made problem on the planet.

Cooperative ownership is becoming more economically efficient than private ownership, and this is primarily due to the explosive advancement of digital technology of the last two decades.

Where blockchain comes in

Blockchain technology is by and large an untapped resource when it comes to solving coordination problems. Over the last ten years or so it has developed a stigma that prevents many from looking more deeply at the core technology. Far beyond simple financial scams, blockchain presents an opportunity for large swathes of people to achieve consensus more cheaply than ever in history.

The technology’s capacity to trustlessly provide voting, governance, data storage, digital provenance, escrow, payment facilitation and much else will revolutionize almost every industry as people begin to realize the applications outside of finance. For marketplace cooperatives especially, it represents the opportunity to build something that can economically outcompete an otherwise-equivalent capitalist business.

By dropping coordination costs while shareholder demands for dividends remain the same, cooperative business structures will edge their way into the market and slowly take over more and more industries.
