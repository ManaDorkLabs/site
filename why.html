The Why

Why cooperatives

Cooperativism vs capitalism: shareholder dividends weighed against cooperative coordination costs.

Cooperativism vs capitalism

Cooperative costs

  • Collective bargaining
  • Meeting overhead
  • Referendum expenses
  • Preference alignment
  • Conflict resolution
  • Capital acquisition

Capitalist costs

  • Management surveillance
  • Compliance and tracking systems
  • Lack of employee ownership limits motivation
  • Investor relations overhead
  • Misalignment between short term and long term gain
  • Dividend payments

The case

Capitalism may have been the dominant market force for the last 200 years, but today it has outlived its usefulness. Hyper concentrated wealth in the hands of a tiny few is an active detriment to the well-being of the many. We believe that cooperatives are the solution to the excessive wealth inequality (and the imbalance of power) that exists today. If we can figure out how to fairly distribute ownership to those who produce the value, rather than that ownership remaining amongst those who simply supplied the upfront capital, then we can begin to start truly solving not only wealth inequality, but in turn almost every human-made problem on the planet.

Fortunately, cooperative ownership is becoming more economically efficient than private ownership, and this is primarily due to the explosive advancement of digital technology of the last two decades.

Blockchain

Blockchain technology is by and large an untapped resource when it comes to solving coordination problems. Over the last 10 years or so, it has developed a stigma that prevents many from looking more deeply at the core technology. Far beyond simple financial scams, blockchain presents an opportunity for large swathes of people to achieve consensus more cheaply than ever in history.

The technology’s capacity to trustlessly provide voting, governance, data storage, digital provenance, escrow, payment facilitation, and many other things WILL revolutionize almost every industry as people begin to realize the applications outside of (degenerate) finance. For marketplace cooperatives especially, blockchain represents the opportunity to build something that can economically outcompete an otherwise-equivalent capitalist business.

By dropping coordination costs while shareholder demands for dividends remain the same, cooperative business structures will edge their way into the market and slowly take over more and more industries.
